# Narrative plan for the manuscript

This note records why the manuscript is ordered the way it is. The target
journal judges four things in order: **methodological increment -> validation ->
reproducibility -> transferability**. The section order follows that sequence
rather than the "pattern -> mechanism -> equity" order a geography reader would
expect.

## 1. Section map

| CEUS section | Content | Why it sits here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Introduction | (i) coverage and capacity are indistinguishable in standard indicators; (ii) what each of the three literatures stops short of; (iii) three numbered contributions | The first sentence must state a defect that can be judged true or false, not a policy background |
| 2 Data and methods | grid, case selection, data sources, six method blocks, **implementation and cost** | A methods section without a cost paragraph reads as "not intended to be reproduced" |
| 3 Validation and sensitivity | conservation residual, dual-pipeline agreement, threshold sweep, collinearity, input quality | The reviewer's first question is "why should I believe this computation" |
| 4 Results | national gradient, regional decoupling, network mechanism, street environment, triple disadvantage, counterfactual | One claim, one table or figure, one paragraph per subsection |
| 5 Discussion | difference from existing practice, boundary conditions, limitations | Not a results recap; only the delta and the caveats |
| 6 Conclusion | answers the three questions in Section 1 | No new numbers, no advocacy |

## 2. Four wording rules

1. **Open on the measurement defect.** Not on the school-allocation policy that
   motivates the case. The policy belongs in Section 2.1, where it explains why
   China is a useful test bed.
2. **Contributions are numbered and falsifiable.** "(i) we separate capacity
   from redistribution by reporting the conservation residual; (ii) we show that
   network geometry and street-level quality act on different margins; (iii) we
   quantify how much inequality survives a counterfactual network upgrade."
3. **Results carry statistics; conclusions carry judgements.** No hedging in the
   conclusion, no unqualified "confirms" in the results.
4. **Conditions instead of recommendations.** Where a lever is binding, say what
   it moves and under which condition, not what a government should do.

## 3. Table and figure budget

Fourteen tables, seven figures, ordered by first mention.

- Kept in the main text: data sources, supply totals with residuals, threshold
  sweep, VIF, regional coverage, linear vs fractional, dominant factors, three
  specifications, education gradient, excess co-occurrence, quadrants, LISA,
  nested regression.
- Dropped from the earlier draft: the coverage table (Fig. 2 carries it), the
  six-city unreached table (Fig. 3 carries it), the scenario table (Fig. 7
  carries it), and the quadrant-share table (Fig. 6b carries it).
- Figures are kept when they show something a table cannot: spatial
  distribution, within-city variation, or the shape of a counterfactual.

## 4. Highlights

Each line is under 85 characters, as the guide requires.

- Faster modes redistribute existing school places rather than adding capacity
- Street-network circuity, not density, drives walking inaccessibility
- Street-view quality forms an independent layer of educational inequality
- Counterfactual scenarios quantify children moved inside the threshold

## 5. Abstract skeleton

Four sentences, one number each, no citations.

1. **Defect** — accessibility indicators conflate how much capacity exists with
   how it is distributed.
2. **Method** — national 1 km G2SFCA (three modes x two stages) plus six-city
   network morphology, eight street-view indicators and a fractional response
   model.
3. **Numbers** — the two hardest results: the 47-point regional spread in
   coverage, and the effect of one standard deviation of circuity.
4. **Implication** — capacity and redistribution are separable; network
   geometry is the second-order lever; street-level quality is an independent
   layer of inequality.
